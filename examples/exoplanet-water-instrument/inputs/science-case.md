## 1. Background

Water vapor is an important indicator of atmospheric composition and chemistry. Transmission spectroscopy can detect wavelength-dependent absorption when a planet passes in front of its host star.

This document is synthetic. Values are selected to exercise the STELA workflow and are not claims about a flight mission or a particular real instrument.

## 2. Science need

Determine whether water vapor is present in nearby temperate exoplanet atmospheres.

Scientists need a repeatable method for distinguishing a water-bearing atmosphere from a no-water hypothesis for a defined target population.

## 3. Science objective

Estimate atmospheric water-vapor abundance for selected temperate exoplanets. Success means detecting water and constraining the retrieved abundance to within one order of magnitude for targets satisfying the adopted brightness and transit assumptions.

## 4. Measurement considerations

### 4.1 Wavelength coverage

The investigation will use a representative water absorption feature centered near 2.7 um. The measured spectrum must cover at least 2.6 through 2.9 um.

### 4.2 Spectral resolution

A resolving power of at least 1000 is the adopted threshold for distinguishing the selected feature from adjacent spectral structure in this example.

### 4.3 Signal-to-noise

The processed spectrum must achieve signal-to-noise of at least 10 per spectral resolution element. This threshold assumes a target-star K magnitude no greater than 9, at least three hours of observation, and observatory pointing jitter no greater than 10 mas.

## 5. Open questions

- The target population and occurrence rates require scientific review.
- Background and systematic noise models remain preliminary.
- Retrieval bias and false-positive probability require validation.
- End-of-life detector degradation has not been included.

These statements are intentionally retained as open questions rather than silently converted into approved requirements.
